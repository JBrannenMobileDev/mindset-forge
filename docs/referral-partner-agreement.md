## 1. What this agreement covers

1.1 The Partner will promote MindsetForge to the Partner's audience using the Partner's own
channels, in the Partner's own voice, on whatever schedule the Partner chooses. There is no minimum
posting requirement, no exclusivity, and no content approval process unless the parties later agree
one in writing.

1.2 In exchange, the Founder will pay the Partner a commission on revenue from subscribers the
Partner refers, calculated and paid as set out below.

1.3 This is a revenue-share referral arrangement only. It is not an employment relationship, an
equity grant, a joint venture, or a legal partnership. See clause 15.

---

## 2. Commission rate and duration

2.1 **Rate.** The Founder will pay the Partner **25% of Net Revenue** from each Referred Subscriber,
as Net Revenue is defined in clause 3.

2.2 **Duration: the lifetime of the subscriber.** Commission is payable for as long as each Referred
Subscriber continues to pay for MindsetForge. There is no 12 month cap, no 24 month cap, and no
other time limit.

2.3 **"Lifetime" means the subscriber's lifetime, not the partnership's lifetime.** This is the
single most important point in this agreement, so it is stated as plainly as possible:

- If the Partner stops posting about MindsetForge, commission on already-referred subscribers
  continues.
- If the Partner and the Founder end this agreement without cause, commission on already-referred
  subscribers continues (clause 12).
- If a Referred Subscriber cancels and later resubscribes to the same account, the resumed payments
  are still commissionable.
- The only things that stop commission on an already-referred subscriber are: the subscriber stops
  paying, or the agreement is terminated for cause under clause 11.

2.4 **Rate changes are forward-looking only.** The Founder may propose a different commission rate
for future referrals, with at least 30 days' written notice. Any new rate applies **only to
subscribers referred on or after the date the new rate takes effect**. Subscribers already referred
stay at the rate that applied when they were referred, permanently. A rate change is never applied
retroactively.

> **Plain-language note on why 2.4 is written this way.** The Founder needs some ability to adjust
> economics as the business changes, but the Partner needs to know that work already done cannot be
> repriced after the fact. Splitting the difference by rate-locking each cohort at the moment of
> referral gives both sides what they actually need.

## 3. Net Revenue: the exact definition

This clause is deliberately the most precise part of the document, because the definition of the
commission base is where revenue-share arrangements most often break down.

3.1 **Formula.** For each payment actually collected from a Referred Subscriber:

```
Net Revenue = Gross Customer Price
            - App Store Commission
            - Taxes
            - Refunds and Chargebacks

Partner Commission = 25% x Net Revenue
```

3.2 **Definitions of each term in the formula.**

(a) **Gross Customer Price** means the amount the subscriber is actually charged for the
subscription, in the currency charged, including any discount, promotional price, or regional price
adjustment that was actually applied. It is the real transaction amount, not the list price.

(b) **App Store Commission** means the fee retained by Apple or Google on that transaction. As of
the effective date, MindsetForge is enrolled in Apple's Small Business Program, so the Apple rate is
currently 15%. Google Play's rate is currently 15% on the first $1 million of annual earnings. If
either store changes its commission rate, or if MindsetForge ceases to qualify for a reduced-rate
program, the actual rate charged by the store on that transaction is the rate used in this formula.
The Founder will tell the Partner in writing within 30 days of becoming aware of any such change.

(c) **Taxes** means sales tax, VAT, GST, digital services tax, or any equivalent transaction tax
collected from or remitted on behalf of the subscriber on that transaction. Where the app store
collects and remits the tax itself and reports proceeds net of tax, that tax is already excluded and
is not deducted a second time. Income taxes on the Founder's own earnings are **not** deducted.

(d) **Refunds and Chargebacks** means amounts returned to the subscriber, or reversed by the store,
the card network, or the payment processor, on a transaction that was previously counted. These are
handled through the clawback mechanism in clause 8.

3.3 **What is expressly NOT deducted.** No deduction is made from the commission base for any of the
following, and this list is illustrative rather than exhaustive:

- Server, hosting, database, or storage costs (for example Firebase or Google Cloud).
- AI model or API costs of any kind, including Anthropic, OpenAI, or any other inference provider,
  regardless of how large those costs become.
- Third-party software, SDK, analytics, monitoring, or subscription tooling.
- Apple or Google developer program fees.
- Payment processing costs other than the app store commission expressly identified in 3.2(b).
- Marketing, advertising, or customer acquisition spend.
- Legal, accounting, insurance, or professional services.
- The Founder's own time, salary, draw, or distributions.
- Any allocation of overhead, general and administrative expense, depreciation, or amortization.

3.4 **The commission base is Net Revenue, not gross profit or net profit.** The Partner's 25% is
calculated on Net Revenue as defined above. Whether MindsetForge is profitable in any given month
does not change the commission owed.

> **Plain-language note on why 3.3 exists, and why it protects both of us.** The three deductions in
> the formula (store commission, taxes, refunds) all share one property: they are money that never
> belongs to MindsetForge in the first place, and every one of them appears as a line item on a
> statement from Apple, Google, or a payment processor. That makes them verifiable by a third party
> rather than asserted by the Founder.
>
> Operating costs are the opposite. They are discretionary, they are set by the Founder alone, and
> they can grow for reasons that have nothing to do with the Partner. If AI costs were deductible,
> the Partner's payout could shrink because the Founder chose a more expensive model, and the Partner
> would have no way to audit that decision. Excluding operating costs means the denominator cannot
> be quietly reduced.
>
> This also protects the Founder. A clean definition tied to store statements means there is nothing
> to argue about, no need to open the Company's books, and no annual negotiation about which costs
> are "really" attributable to referred users. The trade-off is that 25% of Net Revenue is a real
> number the Founder has to be able to afford at scale, which is a deliberate and accepted choice.

## 4. Free trials

4.1 MindsetForge currently offers a 7 day free trial. **No commission accrues on a free trial
period.** Commission accrues only on payments actually collected.

4.2 If a trial converts to a paid subscription, commission begins with the first payment actually
collected and continues for the life of that subscriber under clause 2.

4.3 If a trial never converts, it generates no commission. Trial starts may still be shown on
monthly statements as context, but they create no payment obligation.

4.4 The Founder may change trial length or trial mechanics at any time as a product decision. Doing
so does not change the principle in 4.1.

## 5. Attribution: how a referral is counted, and its known limits

5.1 **Definition of Referred Subscriber.** A user is a Referred Subscriber if **either** of the
following is true:

(a) the user selects "Wes Lowery" in response to the in-app "Who sent you?"
question shown during onboarding; **or**

(b) the Google Play Install Referrer data for that install identifies the Partner's campaign.

Either condition alone is sufficient. Once a user qualifies as a Referred Subscriber, that status is
permanent and is not reassigned later.

5.2 **Honest statement of the limits of attribution.** Purchases happen inside the Apple App Store
and the Google Play Store, which sit between a marketing click and the resulting account. Neither
store passes a per-user identifier back to the app, so it is not technically possible to link every
install deterministically to the click that caused it. Both parties acknowledge this openly rather
than pretending the numbers are exact:

(a) **The counted number is a floor, not a complete census.** Attribution under 5.1 systematically
**undercounts** true referrals. Some users skip the onboarding question, misremember where they
heard about the app, or search the store manually days after seeing a post.

(b) **Realistic capture rates.** The parties' shared working expectation is that capture is
near-total on Android (where the Install Referrer is deterministic) and roughly 70% to 90% on iOS
(where it depends on self-reporting). These are estimates for setting expectations, not warranties.

(c) **The Partner is never penalized for measurement gaps caused by the platforms.** Where a
material and persistent gap appears, clause 6 applies.

5.3 **Second, independent estimate: Apple campaign reporting.** The Partner's link will carry an
Apple campaign token (a `ct=` parameter) so that App Store Connect reports impressions, product page
views, and app units for the Partner's campaign. Both parties may view this reporting on request, and
the Founder will include the relevant figures in monthly statements where available.

5.4 **Apple campaign reporting is not ground truth either.** It is a useful cross-check and nothing
more. Its specific limits, stated so neither party over-relies on it:

(a) It uses a **24 hour attribution window**. A viewer who sees a post and installs three days later
is not counted.

(b) It is **last-touch**. If the user passes through another campaign after the Partner's link, the
other campaign is credited instead.

(c) It only captures users who **tap the link**. Someone who hears about the app and then searches
the App Store manually is invisible to it.

(d) It is **aggregate only**. It reports counts, not identities, so it can never be used to establish
that one specific subscriber was referred, and therefore can never by itself trigger a payment on a
specific account.

5.5 **Neither measurement is treated as authoritative on its own.** Payments are calculated from the
attribution in 5.1, because that is the only method that identifies individual paying accounts.
Apple campaign reporting is used as a sanity check on whether 5.1 is materially undercounting.

## 6. Handling a persistent gap between the two measurements

6.1 The parties will review both data sources **quarterly**, in good faith, with the shared goal of
paying the Partner for the referrals the Partner actually generated rather than only the ones the
platforms happened to record.

6.2 If Apple campaign reporting (or any other credible evidence) indicates that identified referrals
under clause 5.1 are materially undercounting actual referrals over a sustained period, the parties
may agree in writing to apply an **uplift factor** to iOS-attributed commission for future periods
to compensate for the structural undercount.

6.3 **Uplift factor policy: [UPLIFT FACTOR POLICY]** (open decision point, to be agreed before or
shortly after signature). Items to settle here include: what size and duration of gap triggers a
review, what evidence is used, how the uplift is calculated, whether it applies prospectively only,
and how often it is revisited. No specific number is assumed by this draft.

6.4 Any agreed uplift must be recorded in writing and signed by both parties, and applies
prospectively unless the writing says otherwise.

> **Plain-language note.** This clause exists because the honest answer to "can you count every
> referral?" is no, and the Partner should not silently absorb the cost of a platform limitation.
> Deliberately leaving the number blank avoids inventing a formula before either party has real data
> to base one on.

## 7. Statements and payment

7.1 **Cadence.** Commission is calculated monthly on a calendar-month basis and paid **net 30 days
after the close of the month** in which the underlying payments were collected. For example,
commission on payments collected in March is paid by April 30.

7.2 **Monthly statement.** With each payment, the Founder will provide a written statement showing
at minimum:

(a) the number of Referred Subscribers active in the period, and new Referred Subscribers added;

(b) Gross Customer Price collected from those subscribers;

(c) each deduction applied under clause 3, itemised by category (app store commission, taxes,
refunds and chargebacks);

(d) resulting Net Revenue;

(e) the commission calculation (25% x Net Revenue), shown as arithmetic rather than a single total;

(f) any clawback applied under clause 8, and any carried-forward negative balance;

(g) trial starts in the period, marked clearly as non-commissionable; and

(h) available Apple campaign reporting figures for the period, marked clearly as an aggregate
estimate.

7.3 **Minimum payout threshold: [MINIMUM PAYOUT THRESHOLD]** (open decision point). If a month's
commission is below the threshold, the balance carries forward and is paid once the accumulated
balance reaches the threshold. Any accumulated balance below the threshold is paid out in full
regardless of amount when this agreement terminates, and at least once every 12 months.

7.4 **Payment method.** Payments will be made by [PAYMENT METHOD AND PARTNER PAYMENT DETAILS]. Each
party bears its own transfer fees on its own side.

7.5 **Questions and corrections.** The Partner may raise questions about any statement within 90
days of receiving it. The Founder will respond in good faith and provide the underlying app store
payout reports, redacted only as needed to protect other users' or partners' information. Genuine
errors in either direction are corrected in the next statement.

## 8. Refunds, chargebacks, and clawback

8.1 If a payment on which commission was already paid is later refunded or charged back, the
commission attributable to that payment is **clawed back** and netted against the Partner's next
payout.

8.2 Clawbacks are shown as an explicit line item on the statement, identifying the period the
original payment came from.

8.3 **If the balance goes negative.** If clawbacks in a period exceed commission earned in that
period, the negative balance carries forward and is offset against future commission. The Partner is
**not** required to repay cash to the Founder, and the Founder will not invoice the Partner
for a negative balance. If this agreement terminates while a negative balance is outstanding, the
balance is written off and neither party owes the other anything in respect of it.

8.4 Clause 8.3 does not apply where the refunds arise from fraud or abuse by the Partner, which is
handled under clause 11.

> **Plain-language note.** Clawback exists only so that the Partner is not paid on money the Company
> never got to keep. Capping the downside at "future commission" rather than a repayment obligation
> keeps the Partner's worst case at zero rather than negative.

## 9. Assignment, sale, merger, or acquisition

9.1 Neither party may assign this agreement without the other party's written consent, except as set
out in 9.2.

9.2 **On a sale of the business.** If the Founder sells, merges, or otherwise transfers MindsetForge
or substantially all of its assets, the Founder will [OPTION A: require the acquirer to assume this
agreement in full as a condition of closing] / [OPTION B: require the acquirer to assume this
agreement, or, if the acquirer will not assume it, pay the Partner a buyout of
[BUYOUT FORMULA OR MULTIPLE] and terminate the ongoing commission]. The parties must choose one of
these before signature. The obligation cannot simply disappear on a change of control.

9.3 The Partner may not assign or sell the right to receive commission to a third party, but may
direct payment to an entity the Partner controls, on written notice.

9.4 The Partner will be given written notice of any assignment under 9.2 as soon as
confidentiality obligations reasonably permit.

> **Plain-language note, and a specific flag for the lawyer.** An uncapped, perpetual revenue-share
> on a defined cohort of subscribers is a real balance-sheet item, and it will surface in diligence
> in any future fundraise or acquisition. Buyers and investors will want to know exactly what it
> costs, how long it runs, and whether it can be terminated or bought out. The Founder should work
> through the assignment mechanics with a lawyer before signing, including: whether a buyout right
> exists and at what multiple, whether the obligation is capped in an acquisition scenario, how it is
> disclosed, and whether the Partner has any consent or notice rights. Leaving this clause vague is
> the single most likely way this agreement causes a problem years from now, which is why both
> options are spelled out rather than glossed over.

## 10. Partner obligations

10.1 **FTC disclosure on every promotion.** The Partner must clearly and conspicuously disclose the
material connection to MindsetForge in **every** promotion, in line with the FTC Endorsement Guides.
Acceptable forms include "#ad", "paid partnership", "sponsored", or a clear spoken or written
statement that the Partner earns a commission. Disclosure must be:

(a) visible without tapping "more", expanding a caption, or scrolling;

(b) present in the video or audio itself where the promotion is video or audio, not only in the
description; and

(c) in the same language as the promotion.

Burying the disclosure in a bio, a link-in-bio page, a hashtag block, or a comment does not satisfy
this clause.

10.2 **Accurate description of the product and its pricing.** The Partner must not misrepresent what
MindsetForge does, and must not misstate subscription price, billing frequency, trial length, or how
to cancel. Both Apple's and Google's rules prohibit misrepresenting subscription pricing or terms,
and inaccurate claims put the app's store listing at risk, which would harm both parties.

10.3 **No health, medical, or therapeutic claims.** MindsetForge is a mindset and coaching product,
not a medical device or a treatment. The Partner must not claim it diagnoses, treats, cures, or
prevents any medical or mental health condition, and must not present it as a substitute for therapy
or professional care.

10.4 **No incentivised, misleading, or artificial referrals.** The Partner must not: offer cash or
cash-equivalent rebates for signing up; use bots, click farms, or automated installs; sign up under
the Partner's own or an associate's account to generate commission; use spam email, spam DMs, or
unsolicited bulk messaging; run paid search or app store ads on MindsetForge brand terms without
written permission; or use the Founder's or MindsetForge's name in a way that implies the Partner
speaks for the Company.

10.5 **Brand and trademark use.** The Partner may use the MindsetForge name, logo, and approved
screenshots to promote the app, and only to promote the app. The Founder may ask for a specific
piece of content to be corrected or removed if it is inaccurate or breaches this clause, and the
Partner will do so promptly.

10.6 **Compliance generally.** The Partner is responsible for complying with applicable advertising,
consumer protection, and platform rules in the jurisdictions the Partner posts from, and with the
terms of the social platforms the Partner uses.

## 11. Termination for cause and forfeiture

11.1 The Founder may terminate this agreement immediately, on written notice, for cause. "Cause"
means any of the following:

(a) fraud, including fabricated, incentivised, or artificially generated referrals;

(b) self-referral, or referrals through accounts controlled by or coordinated with the Partner for
the purpose of generating commission;

(c) spam, bots, click farms, or other prohibited promotion methods under clause 10.4;

(d) material misrepresentation of the product, its pricing, its trial terms, or its results,
including claims prohibited by clauses 10.2 and 10.3;

(e) conduct by the Partner that causes material damage to the MindsetForge brand, including public
conduct unrelated to the app that would make continued association materially harmful; or

(f) any other material breach of this agreement that is not cured within 14 days of written notice.

11.2 **Forfeiture.** On termination for cause, the Partner **forfeits all accrued but unpaid
commission and all future commission**, including commission on subscribers previously referred. The
Founder may also recover commission already paid on referrals shown to be fraudulent.

11.3 Before terminating for cause, the Founder will describe the specific conduct in writing and,
except in cases of clear fraud, give the Partner a reasonable opportunity to respond. The intent is
to resolve misunderstandings rather than to look for a reason not to pay.

> **Plain-language note.** Forfeiture is a severe remedy, and it is limited to a short and specific
> list of things that are genuinely damaging: fake referrals, spam, lying about the product, and
> serious brand harm. It is not a general dissatisfaction clause, and it does not apply to slow
> posting, low results, or a decision to stop promoting. Those are covered by clause 12, where the
> Partner keeps everything already earned.

## 12. Termination without cause, and what survives

12.1 Either party may terminate this agreement for any reason on 30 days' written notice.

12.2 **What survives termination without cause.** Commission on subscribers already referred before
the termination date **continues to accrue and continues to be paid**, for as long as those
subscribers keep paying, at the rate that applied when each was referred. Termination stops new
referrals from being credited. It does not switch off the existing ones.

12.3 After termination without cause, the Founder continues to provide monthly statements and
payments under clause 7 for as long as commission remains payable, and the Partner's confidentiality
obligations under clause 14 continue.

12.4 The Partner must stop using MindsetForge branding for active promotion after termination,
though the Partner is not required to delete historical posts.

12.5 The following clauses survive termination in all cases: 3 (Net Revenue definition), 7
(statements and payment), 8 (clawback), 9 (assignment), 12 (survival), 14 (confidentiality), 15
(relationship of the parties), 16 (tax), and 17 (governing law).

## 13. Tax and independent contractor status

13.1 **W-9 before first payment.** The Partner must provide a completed IRS Form W-9 (or Form W-8
series if the Partner is a non-US person) before the first payment is made. No payment will be
issued without it.

13.2 **1099-NEC.** The Founder will issue an IRS Form 1099-NEC to the Partner for any calendar year
in which payments to the Partner total $600 or more, as required by law.

13.3 **The Partner is responsible for the Partner's own taxes.** No income tax, self-employment tax,
or withholding is deducted from commission payments unless required by law. Commission is paid gross
to the Partner.

13.4 **Independent contractor.** The Partner performs services as an independent contractor. The
Partner is not an employee of MindsetForge or of the Founder, and is not entitled to employee
benefits, insurance, paid leave, expense reimbursement, or equity of any kind. The Partner controls
the manner, method, timing, and content of the Partner's own promotional work.

## 14. Confidentiality

14.1 The monthly statements, revenue figures, subscriber counts, pricing arrangements, and any other
non-public business information the Founder shares under this agreement are **confidential**. The
Partner will not disclose them publicly or to third parties.

14.2 The Partner may share this information with the Partner's own accountant, tax preparer, lawyer,
manager, or agent, provided those people are bound to keep it confidential.

14.3 The Partner may state publicly that the Partner has a paid partnership with MindsetForge, and
in fact must disclose it under clause 10.1. The Partner may not publish specific revenue figures,
subscriber counts, or commission amounts.

14.4 Confidentiality survives termination and continues for 3 years after the last payment under
this agreement.

14.5 The Founder will likewise keep the Partner's tax information, payment details, and any
non-public audience or performance data confidential, and will not use them for any purpose other
than performing this agreement.

## 15. Relationship of the parties

15.1 The word "partner" is used in this agreement in its everyday, commercial sense, meaning
someone the Founder works with. It does not carry its legal meaning.

15.2 **No partnership, joint venture, agency, franchise, or employment relationship is created by
this agreement.** Neither party is the agent or legal representative of the other. Neither party may
incur obligations, make commitments, sign contracts, or make representations on the other's behalf.
Neither party owes the other fiduciary duties. There is no sharing of losses, no joint ownership of
assets, no ownership interest in MindsetForge, and no right to participate in the management or
control of MindsetForge.

15.3 The Partner acquires no intellectual property rights in MindsetForge, its name, its content,
its models, or its user data. The Founder acquires no rights in the Partner's channels, content, or
audience.

15.4 Each party is responsible for its own costs, taxes, insurance, and compliance.
